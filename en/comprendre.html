Android For your device
Chrome: tap ⋮ → Delete browsing data (or ⋮ → History).

iPhone or iPad For your device

Chrome, Edge, Firefox: press Ctrl + Shift + Delete.
Mac For your device
Chrome, Edge, Firefox: press ⌘ + Shift + Delete.

If clearing all your history might draw attention, delete only the pages from this site. Private browsing does not save your history.

Understanding resources

You don't need to know exactly what you need before contacting a resource. A support line can guide you.

By needBy regionFull directoryUnderstanding resources

What's the difference between…

A support line

A confidential phone service to talk to someone, ask questions, vent and be safely pointed to the right resources. Often open 24/7, sometimes also by text or chat.

See these resources →

A women's centre

A daytime community space offering listening, one-on-one support, activities and a support network to break isolation. You don't need to live there or to have left your partner.

See these resources →

A first-stage (emergency) shelter

A safe place available immediately, 24/7, for women and their children fleeing domestic violence. Stays are short (a few weeks) in shared spaces. The address is kept confidential.

See these resources →

A second-stage (transition) shelter

Takes over after a first-stage shelter. It offers individual, self-contained apartments for up to 2 years, to rebuild safely away from post-separation harassment.

See these resources →

Outreach services

Many shelters offer meetings, follow-up and groups to women who don't live there. You can get their help while staying at home.

See these resources →

A legal resource

Free information and advice about your rights and options: complaints, protection orders, separation, child custody. Contacting them does not commit you to anything, including filing a complaint.

See these resources →

A CAVAC

Crime Victims Assistance Centres offer emotional support and information about the court process, whether or not you have filed a complaint.

See these resources →

IVAC

Québec's crime victims compensation program can cover some costs, such as therapy or lost income. A police report is not required to apply.

See these resources →

A sexual violence resource

A specialized service that welcomes, believes and supports people affected by sexual violence, recent or past, while respecting their choices.

See these resources →

You can ask for help even if…

  • you're not sure the situation counts as violence;
  • you don't want to leave your partner;
  • you just want to talk to someone;
  • you're looking for information for someone close to you.
Talk to someone now

Can't find what you need, or want to talk to someone? Call SOS violence conjugale at 1 800 363-9010, available 24/7.
